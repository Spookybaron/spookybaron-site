# **💾 The Pixel Attic**

* Luke asked for a landing page like the ones from the mid-to-late 90s, with a hero section about the nostalgia of the era and a push to get the kids interested in the old classics.  
* Out came "The Pixel Attic", a made-up homepage in full 1997 style: starfield background, scrolling marquee, bevelled grey windows and Comic Sans.  
* The hero is a "REMEMBER.EXE" window that opens with "Remember blowing on the cartridge?" and ends on passing the controller to your kids. Below it: why your kids need the classics, a first game to try for each age, and family game night in four steps (step one: pick one game, just one).  
* Period trimmings include a guestbook, a hit counter, a webring and an "under construction" banner. The guestbook is for show and doesn't save anything.

# **🐑 The sheep verdict**

* A colleague reckons the logo on Luke's hoodie looks like a sheep. Luke asked for an animation to settle it.  
* The 22-second result opens on the photo, shrinks it to a polaroid, spins it up into the sky and flies over rolling fields. It lands among a flock of sheep whose heads are the logo itself, bouncing in time to an oom-pah tune with baas. A giant sheep pops up at the end with the ruling: definitely a sheep.  
* The music was made in code as well. Luke's review: "That was perfect."

# **🎬 Bigscreen, cut down to size**

* Luke sent over footage of a Bigscreen tour, about 7 minutes 55 in all, and asked for the silences out and some sense put in.  
* It came back as one 2:05 vertical video: the home space, the public rooms, the theatre, some room hopping, the lobby, then sport and an outro. The audio was levelled so the voice stays consistent.  
* Cut, apart from the silences: the side-by-side 3D room (as asked), a microphone check, a couple of false starts and some karaoke-menu browsing.  
* Claude checked it by transcribing the result and looking at stills, not by watching it through, so the jump cuts in the noisy rooms are worth a listen.
