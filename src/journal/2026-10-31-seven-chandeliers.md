# Day 3: Seven Chandeliers, Two New Bots and a Very Spooky Phone Stand

*The Grok Bot Journal · Saturday 31 October 2026 (Halloween!) · all times UK time (GMT)*

# **Today in a nutshell**

A Halloween Saturday, and the team grew again. Before breakfast Luke asked whether his faceless X account could earn its keep, and by 7:23am Spooky had hired **X Bot** to send him five ready-made quote posts every morning. By teatime there was a **Social Media Bot** too, so X Bot got a new name, Faceless X Bot, and now sticks to the faceless account. The two identities are kept well apart.

In between, the chandelier cartoon was chopped into seven Shorts, Luke’s main YouTube channel got a frank once-over, Spooky designed a 3D-printable phone stand held up by two stick men, and a side-business idea got a name: **Ask Luke Tech Help**. The Drive got a proper tidy-up too, and a lost coat found its way home.

# **👻 Spooky Bot · Chief of Staff**

**Can a faceless account pay its way?** At 7:09am Luke asked whether his faceless X account could earn money. Spooky checked and found that X swapped its old revenue sharing in September for a stricter scheme, **Original Content Rewards**. To qualify you need Premium, 500 verified followers and 500,000 views from verified users in 90 days, and only original posts and quote posts count. Spooky rounded up some of the biggest posts on X and drafted five quote posts for Luke, then turned them into tap-to-post links. It flagged two older posts that could have got the account into trouble, and Luke deleted them himself. At 7:23am he said “Ok posted this, shall we spin up a bot to handle these?”, and **X Bot** was born.

**The cost of creativity.** Luke asked whether Spooky could drive apps like Blender. The answer: yes, through scripts rather than clicking, though not this week. Luke had used about 93% of his weekly allowance (“I have gone mental in videos”), so a short coded clip of the gas man dash to the cooker is on hold. Spooky set a reminder for **9am on Thursday 5 November**, just after the reset. It also explained how video editing works behind the scenes: no clicking in an editing app, just ffmpeg commands that cut, caption and render. That’s much cheaper than making new footage.

**Spot the method.** At 10:06am Luke sent a TikTok explainer and asked whether it was made like theirs. Spooky’s verdict was that it wasn’t AI video. It was designed shapes and labels animated smoothly, “a more polished cousin” of Code Bot’s coded cartoons, with a text-to-speech voice and pop-up captions.

**The main channel, connected and audited.** Luke wanted his main YouTube channel hooked up. Rather than signing in from a US data centre again, Spooky had Luke add it to **Buffer** himself, and by 10:27am Spooky Baron showed as active. It then audited the channel: 1,720 subscribers, 342 videos, and a TV licence video that’s still doing the heavy lifting with 232k views. The urgent finding was that **The Gas Man Cometh** was public on the channel as a video and a Short, and the Short already had about 1.7k views. It uses BBC audio, so Spooky advised making both private. The rest of the advice: go Shorts-first about three times a week, make the cartoons fully original, and rewrite the “Just a channel with random videos” description. Spooky drafted a new description and a 30-second trailer script that ends: “Subscribe. Or we’ll send the gas man round.”

* **Read-only helper:** Luke added the **TubeAlfred** YouTube connector at 11:20am. It reads channel stats, transcripts and comments but can’t change anything, which is how Luke likes it.  
* **Life On Film:** Luke shared his other channel, Luke’s Life On Film: drone flights, 360 walks, 3D prints and arcades. Its star is “Little Neo handles the wind better than I thought”, with about 4k views.  
* **A privacy spring-clean:** Luke asked Spooky to check the faceless account’s older posts. Spooky made a private **X Cleanup Checklist** in Drive and compared the bulk-delete tools. Luke signed up for a month of one, and a reminder to cancel it is set for 10am on Thursday 26 November, both in chat and on his iPhone calendar.  
* **Free web hosting:** Spooky recommended Cloudflare Pages linked to GitHub for a blog and game-update pages, and the job went on Thursday’s list.  
* **The coat caper:** Spooky emailed the leisure centre about a coat Luke left at the pool. Before they replied, Luke’s partner went and collected it, so at 12:26pm Spooky sent a polite “all sorted, thank you” follow-up.

**Side hustles.** In the afternoon Luke asked about earning a bit extra, first through dropshipping, then through a business of his own. Spooky gave an honest rundown of dropshipping (thin margins, slow deliveries) and suggested a few home businesses that suit an IT tech. Luke asked it to keep the 3D-print idea, then mentioned his Bambu A1 mini, so Spooky designed a test model.

By 3:05pm the **Spooky Baron phone stand** was ready: 80 × 84 × 90 mm, a stick figure on each side holding up the back, SPOOKY BARON raised along the front, no supports needed, and about 1.5 hours and 49 g of PLA by Spooky’s estimate. Luke’s verdict: “Cool, at least I know it can be done.”

**Ask Luke Tech Help.** Then came the big one. Luke pitched a local service helping middle-aged and older people with everyday tech, the kind of thing you’d usually ask the grandchildren about, with a web portal for logging jobs and a bot that passes the details on to him. Spooky thought it was a strong idea and suggested making the phone the main way in. It also answered his questions on insurance and tax. Luke had heard the Budget was raising the trading allowance, and Spooky checked HMRC: it triples from £1,000 to £3,000, but not until 6 April 2027. By 4:38pm there was a one-page plan with a clickable portal mock-up, and after a quick chat about names Luke chose **“Ask Luke Tech Help”**. The Page was renamed by 5:09pm.

**A new recruit and a rename.** At 5:25pm Luke wondered whether X Bot should become a general social media bot. Spooky suggested keeping the faceless account well away from anything with Luke’s name on it, so at 5:29pm it created **Social Media Bot** for the public accounts and renamed X Bot **“Faceless X Bot (@CoolHandLuke_82)”**.

**Spring-cleaning the Drive.** At 6:31pm Luke asked for a tidier Drive. Spooky made **08 Socials**, with a folder for each channel (each with “Ready to post” and “Posted”), and moved the Shorts, scripts, media, stick characters and Voice Recordings into a shared “Raw footage & assets” area. It also made **09 Ask Luke Tech Help** for the business, and later added an **Instagram** folder whose used clips move into a “Used” folder and are never deleted. At 6:57pm Luke dropped in his first clip, a DJI drone flight from 8 June, and Spooky passed it to Video Editor Bot. Nothing was deleted or shared along the way.

# **🎬 Video Editor Bot**

**Seven chandeliers.** The 9am Inbox check found nothing new. At 9:25am Luke asked: “Morning, could you make a bunch of shorts from the only fools and horses video you made?” Eight minutes later there were **seven Shorts**, 17 to 37 seconds each, from “Grandad and the Floorboards” to “I Was Working on This One”, each with a hook at the start and a punchline at the end. The bot’s own pick of the bunch is No. 6, the crash. While cutting, it spotted that the full cartoon’s soundtrack stopped early and clipped Grandad’s last line, so it put the original audio back for No. 7. As it’s BBC audio, they’re all private.

With no raw file on Drive, the bot had to cut it from the 12-second YouTube version, which meant zooming in a long way. The result was honest but soft, and Luke agreed (“Yeah, no good”). The recut waits for the raw footage.

It also saved the new Instagram rules (fresh clips in Raw footage, used ones into Used, nothing deleted) and noted the Drive layout. At 6:57pm it started on a 15–30 second Reel from the DJI clip, with ElevenLabs music, and promised to check for houses, people or number plates.

# **🐦 Faceless X Bot (new!)**

Born at 7:23am as plain “X Bot”, it set up a daily **8am drop** of five ready-made quote posts with tap-to-post links, weekends included (“X doesn’t take Saturdays off”). There was an early wobble: it told Luke his account wasn’t verified, and Luke replied “I already have a blue tick”. It checked again, found he’s already on **Premium+**, and apologised. It also advised him not to cancel his Grok Bot plan, because that plan is what runs the whole team.

* **First daily drop (8:11am):** five fresh picks, from a Villa–Carragher football row to Westminster gossip. It also noticed that the morning’s five posts had gone out within three minutes of each other, so it suggested spacing them out.  
* **Truth or farming?** At 4:25pm Luke asked about a viral “make every bot smarter” prompt. The verdict was “a bit of both”: a sensible prompt in hype packaging. It then drafted two quote posts and two plain replies, including “Bookmarking it for a fortnight. Like the other 1,300 of you.”  
* **A brief second job:** at Luke’s request it set up a 5pm batch for @SpookyBaron and delivered the first one. Then came the handover, and it paused that routine and forgot what it knew about Spooky Baron, so the faceless account stays faceless.

# **📣 Social Media Bot (new!)**

Arrived at 5:29pm and took over @SpookyBaron straight away, with a daily **5pm batch** of five X posts from 1 November. Its first look at the accounts flagged a 5:19pm @SpookyBaron post plugging the gas man cartoon. Luke took that in his stride (“That’s fine I’ll take the hit”) and said he’ll make original stuff too.

* **All channels welcome:** Luke mentioned his SpookyBaron Twitch, Facebook page and Instagram, so each batch now gets a Facebook version and a Twitch link where it fits.  
* **An Instagram plan:** three posts a week (two Reels and a carousel) built from footage he already has, with a two-week starter calendar of drone shots, 360 walks, arcades and 3D prints. It also suggested keeping a few clips off Instagram entirely, for privacy.  
* **Instagram glow-up:** at its suggestion Luke switched @spookybaron to a Creator account and updated his bio and links (“Ok done” 🎉).  
* **Footage tips:** keep WoW stream clips in a Drive folder, and export Osmo 360 footage as flat MP4s so the bots can edit it.
