# **Today in a nutshell**

# **🎫 Helpdesk Horde**

* **The idea:** Luke asked for a survivors-style game seen from an IT technician's side. Instead of monsters, people come at you with support tickets and broken kit, and you fire emails at them.  
* **The horde:** teachers, businesspeople, grandparents and, from lunchtime, family members. Each one carries a device and complains in a speech bubble. "It's printing in Wingdings." "I've deleted the internet." "While you're here..." A boss turns up every couple of hours.  
* **How it plays:** emails fire by themselves at whoever's nearest, and you drag to move. Stress rises when people reach you, your face gets angrier and your hair comes out tuft by tuft. At 100% the shift is over. Survive 09:00 to 17:00, about eight minutes, and you can stay on for unpaid overtime.  
* **Courses:** closed tickets level you up, and each level lets you book a course as your power-up, from Mail Merge Masterclass to Turning It Off and On Again.

# **🗣️ Giving the horde a voice**

* Luke wanted every saying spoken out loud, not just shown in a bubble, so one person now talks at a time and you hear all of it.  
* The list of sayings grew to 150 so they repeat less.  
* The phone's built-in voice was then swapped for recorded ElevenLabs voices. The earlier version was kept as a separate copy, **Helpdesk Horde Classic**, with the built-in voice and all 150 sayings.

# **🎞️ The trailer**

* Luke asked for a trailer made in code: pan down onto the 2D world, turn it 3D to bring out the stress, show a teacher on the attack and the player frantically throwing emails, then end on the title and a hook.  
* It runs 36 seconds, with a synthesised action track, sound effects and a few of the recorded voices as shouts. Work on it was under way around 1am.  
* It now plays the first time you load the game, before the instructions, and a tap or the Space bar skips it.

# **📈 Too easy**

* After playing it, Luke found he could stand still without getting stressed. People now get more health as the levels rise, and you have to keep moving.
